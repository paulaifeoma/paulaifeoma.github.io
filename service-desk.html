Service Desk Operations & Performance Report

A 2024 service desk dashboard connecting ticket demand, recurring incidents, root causes, repair costs, and SLA performance to support operational review and prioritisation.

Project Overview

This analysis used ticket-level data to examine SLA performance, resolution times, recurring incidents and customer satisfaction, with the aim of identifying operational patterns and areas for improvement.

Analytical Approach

I created a three-page Power BI dashboard using a demonstration dataset.
It presents a 2024 view of service desk workload, incidents, recurring issues, repair costs, SLA performance, and customer satisfaction.

My contribution

I prepared the data, built the data model and measures, and designed the dashboard.

Tools: Power BI Desktop, Power Query, DAX

    SLA Performance and Customer Experience Dashboard

    Operational performance focuses on how effectively the service desk handles incoming tickets. The key performance measure is the Service Level Agreement (SLA), which establishes the expected service standard for resolving a ticket.
    62.85% of tickets with a 4-hour SLA met target; all tickets with a 24-hour SLA met target.

    Customer satisfaction provides a second perspective on service performance. The average score of 3.0/5 remained relatively stable throughout 2024, ranging from approximately 2.9 to 3.1 across the months.
    This suggests that customer experience was relatively consistent, but the overall score leaves room for improvement. Future analysis could examine whether satisfaction is associated with resolution time, SLA breaches, agent performance, or specific ticket characteristics.

    Key Insights

    In 2024, the Service Desk handled 10,000 tickets, comprising 5,644 service requests and 4,356 incidents.
    While the average resolution time was 3.3 hours, overall SLA compliance was 70%, meaning approximately three in ten tickets did not meet their applicable service-level target.
    Recurring problems accounted for 31% of recorded incidents, while average customer satisfaction was 3.0 out of 5. These findings highlighted three key areas for analysis: reducing recurring problems, identifying high-impact root causes, and improving SLA performance while maintaining a positive customer experience.

    Project Outcome

    This service desk report presents the 2024 workload, incident trends, repair costs, root causes, SLA performance, and customer satisfaction. The findings identified SLA compliance, recurring incidents, high-impact assets, repair costs and customer satisfaction as interconnected areas requiring attention.
    In particular, the 31% level of recurring incidents and 70% SLA compliance indicated opportunities to address underlying problems rather than focusing solely on resolving individual tickets. The reports presented in this project enabled management to move from simply monitoring ticket volumes to identifying recurring issues, problematic assets and areas where targeted interventions could improve performance.

    The recommended actions are reducing recurring incidents through root-cause analysis, improving SLA performance through workload balancing and escalation controls, prioritising high-cost configuration issues, reviewing problematic assets, and addressing factors contributing to lower customer satisfaction.
    Overall, the project demonstrates how service desk data can be transformed into actionable operational intelligence to support performance improvement, cost control, proactive incident management and a better customer experience.
